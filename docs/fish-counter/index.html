    How it works and tips
    1. Each frame is scaled down and compared with a slowly updating background model of the scene.
    2. Pixels whose color differs by more than the threshold are marked as moving. Isolated noise pixels are removed, and nearby fragments are merged into groups.
    3. Tracking links groups from frame to frame, predicting where each fish will be from its speed and direction.
    4. A new group is only an unconfirmed detection (thin dashed box) until it has persisted and actually moved. Flickers, reflections and noise that appear and disappear are discarded.
    5. Confirmed fish get an ID, a color and a path. If a fish is lost inside the view, it is counted as hidden (dashed circle) at its last position. If it swims out through an edge, it is counted as out of view.
    6. When a fish appears again near where a hidden fish was lost, or enters through an edge while fish are out of view, it gets the old ID back instead of a new one. The dashed part of a path shows the gap.
    7. Fish (tracked) = visible + hidden + out of view. Fish not seen for longer than the memory settings are dropped.
    • Keep the phone fixed. Any camera movement looks like motion everywhere, and the background then resets automatically. Fish being tracked are remembered during the reset.
    • Use Select area to exclude reflections, the frame and the room outside the glass. The edges of the selected area count as the edges of the view.
    • If one fish is counted twice after hiding, increase Remember hidden fish. If the total stays too high after fish have really gone, decrease it.
    • Fish cannot be told apart by appearance. When two fish cross or swap places while hidden, their IDs can swap, but the total is unaffected.
    • A continuous stream of bubbles moves like a fish and can be tracked. Exclude bubblers with Select area.
    • Paths CSV holds each fish's position 10 times per second while the log runs, with x and y as fractions of the frame (0 to 1).
